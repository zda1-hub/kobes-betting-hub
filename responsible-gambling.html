What are warning signs of gambling harm?

Look beyond money won or lost. Gambling can affect finances, sleep, mood, work and relationships.

  • Trying to recover losses by continuing to gamble.
  • Hiding gambling or money problems from people close to you.
  • Borrowing money or missing bills because of gambling.
  • Finding it difficult to stop or spending longer than intended.
  • Missing responsibilities or activities you previously enjoyed.

This is an awareness checklist, not a diagnosis. You do not need to wait for every sign to appear before seeking support. The Responsible Gambling Council explains these warning signs.

Limits, time-outs and self-exclusion: what is the difference?

Account limits

Tools can limit deposits, time or other activity. Check exactly what each limit covers; a deposit limit and a loss limit are different measures. Limits do not make gambling risk-free.

Time-out

A temporary account break. Its duration and scope depend on the operator and jurisdiction.

Self-exclusion

A request to be excluded from specified gambling services. Coverage and duration vary; closing an account is not necessarily the same process.

Check your regulator’s current instructions rather than assuming one request covers every site, app or venue. Blocking tools can add a barrier but are not a guarantee or a replacement for support. Read the UK Gambling Commission’s explanation; its rules apply to its jurisdiction, not automatically to the U.S.

Where can I find gambling support?

  • United States: the National Problem Gambling Helpline connects people with resources. Loved ones can also contact it.
  • Local resources: use NCPG’s help-by-state directory for your location.
  • Outside the U.S.: contact your local problem-gambling service or regulator; program coverage differs.

Sports betting should never come before financial security, health, work or relationships. Avoid chasing losses and never use money needed for essentials.
