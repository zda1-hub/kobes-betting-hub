ARBITRAGE GUIDE

What is sports betting arbitrage?

Sports betting arbitrage describes a price difference between sportsbooks that can produce a theoretical positive return across mutually exclusive outcomes. Real-world execution can fail: changing odds, rejected bets and different settlement rules can turn an apparent opportunity into a loss.

What does arbitrage mean in sports betting?+

A two-way sports betting arbitrage opportunity appears when the best available price on each mutually exclusive outcome produces a combined implied probability below 100%. These implied probabilities are derived from prices, not measured chances of winning. It is a pricing condition, not permission to call every attempt a guaranteed win.

The critical conditionBoth sides must be placed at the displayed odds, for the required amounts, before either price changes. A rejection, limit, void, settlement difference, or partial fill can leave you exposed.
How the calculation works+

Convert each side’s odds to decimal odds. Divide 1 by each decimal price and add the results. If the total is below 1.00, the displayed pair is theoretically an arbitrage.

For positive American odds, decimal odds = 1 + (American odds ÷ 100). For negative American odds, decimal odds = 1 + (100 ÷ the absolute American odds). For a two-outcome market, the outcomes must cover every possible result under matching settlement rules.

“Mutually exclusive” means both outcomes cannot happen together; “exhaustive” means no possible result is left out. See OpenStax’s probability terminology. The odds conversion and return calculation below are our own worked explanation.

Arbitrage test = (1 ÷ decimal odds A) + (1 ÷ decimal odds B)
Theoretical return on total stake = (1 ÷ arbitrage test) − 1
A $190.48 example+

Imagine a Lakers–Celtics moneyline market with exactly two outcomes and matching settlement rules. One sportsbook lists the Lakers at +110; another lists the Celtics at +110. These are hypothetical prices, not a live opportunity.

Book 1: Lakers +110 — stake $95.24
Book 2: Celtics +110 — stake $95.24
Total wager: $190.48
Either winning side pays $95.24 × 2.10 = $200.00 after rounding
Projected profit: $200.00 − $190.48 = $9.52 (about 5%)

The payout includes the winning stake; it is not all profit. Each exact payout is $200.004, shown as $200.00 to the nearest cent. This simplified example excludes fees and taxes and assumes normal winning/losing settlement on both sides.

The math works only if both bets are accepted for the full amounts at +110 and the market truly has two outcomes with matching rules. If an odd changes, a bet is limited or rejected, or a wager is voided, the projected profit can disappear and one side may be left exposed.

Why is sports betting arbitrage not risk-free?+
  • Line movement: sportsbooks update prices as new information and money arrive.
  • Latency: an odds feed can be slightly behind the sportsbook’s checkout screen.
  • Limits: one book may accept less than the amount needed to balance the other side.
  • Market mismatch: overtime, pushes, player participation, and void rules may differ.
  • Acceptance risk: the first wager can be accepted while the second is rejected or repriced.

For a concrete operator example, DraftKings explains that voided wagers are canceled and their stakes refunded, including some unplayed-event and participation cases. A refund on one side does not preserve the example’s two-sided payout. Other operators and markets can have different rules.

Britain’s Gambling Commission explains that businesses can decline bets and that terms matter. That guidance concerns its Great Britain remit; it is not a statement of US law.

What a complete record needs+
  • The exact event, participants and market, including overtime, push and participation rules.
  • The quoted odds and timestamp, alongside the actual accepted odds and amounts for each side.
  • Both confirmation receipts, plus any rejection, partial acceptance or void notice.
  • The final settlement of both wagers, with refunded stakes and any costs recorded separately.

A quote or an unsubmitted slip is not proof of an accepted wager. DraftKings’ betting glossary distinguishes the slip from the confirmed bet receipt. A calculated return should remain labeled theoretical until the full record supports a settled result.

Common questions about arbitrage+

Does an arbitrage alert guarantee a profit?

No. An alert describes quoted prices at a point in time. It does not confirm that every wager will be accepted or settled under identical rules. A displayed theoretical return is different from a completed, settled result.

Is arbitrage the same as predicting a winner?

No. The concept concerns differences in prices across outcomes, rather than a prediction that one team or player will win. That distinction does not remove execution risk or make sports betting risk-free.

Why can the final result differ from the advertised return?

Prices can change, stakes can be limited, and markets can have different overtime, participation or void rules. Fees and taxes may also affect the final net result. A percentage shown in an example is not a promise of earnings.
