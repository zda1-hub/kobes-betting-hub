WRITEUP CHECKLIST

A pick should be reproducible.

A useful writeup lets another reader identify the same wager, understand the reasoning, and judge the result without filling in missing details.

By Kobe’s Betting Hub · Page updated October 22, 2026 · Sources checked October 22, 2026

On this page

  • Checklist
  • Statistical context
  • Grading
  • Annotated example
  • Sources and scope
The seven essentials+
  1. Event and participant: identify the game, team, or player without ambiguity.
  2. Exact market: moneyline, spread, total, player prop, or another defined market.
  3. Line and odds: include both the threshold and price, such as over 24.5 at -110.
  4. Sportsbook or source: show where the quoted number was available.
  5. Timestamp: state when the number was checked or the pick was released.
  6. Rationale: explain the strongest supporting evidence and the main uncertainty.
  7. Result: grade the posted line consistently as a win, loss, push, or void.

This seven-part checklist is the Hub’s editorial method, not a regulator’s reporting standard. DraftKings’ glossary distinguishes a pre-confirmation slip from a confirmed receipt and defines settlement outcomes. Those definitions support the record distinctions here; another operator’s rules can differ.

Relevant statistics need context+

“Hit in the last 4/4” can be useful, but it is not a complete argument. Ask whether the role, minutes, opponent, injuries, location, and line were comparable. Small samples can describe recent events without proving what will happen next. OpenStax’s probability terminology explains why a short run can differ from a long-run expectation. See also mean versus median and per-game versus per-minute context.

A teaser should not reveal a fake certainty.A free preview can show a relevant trend and a concise research summary while keeping the full play for members. It should not hide a weak rationale behind “lock” language.
Separate fact from interpretation+

A fact might be that a player exceeded a number in four recent games. An interpretation is that the trend will continue because the projected role and matchup are similar. Labeling that distinction makes the reasoning easier to challenge and improve.

Record the number that was posted+

If a player was released over 14.5 but closes at 16.5, grade the official record at 14.5. Readers who arrive later should be told the original number moved rather than being encouraged to treat a materially different wager as identical.

Red flags+
  • No visible line, odds, or timestamp.
  • A result graded using a better number than members received.
  • Only successful trends or winning days are retained.
  • Anonymous “inside information” presented without support.
  • Certainty language that ignores injuries, variance, and line movement.
  • A larger stake suggested mainly because the last play lost.
An annotated record, not a live pick+

Entirely hypothetical: Player A, Team A versus Team B; over 14.5 points at -110; Example Sportsbook; checked October 22, 2026 at 10:00 AM MST. These are invented names and terms, not a recommendation.

  • Evidence: identify the actual data source, date range, sample size and relevant role changes. “Four recent games” alone is incomplete.
  • Interpretation: label assumptions about future minutes or role as assumptions, not confirmed facts.
  • Update: if a later quote is 16.5, keep the original 14.5 and timestamp visible rather than silently replacing them.
  • Settlement: a hypothetical final result of 14 points is a loss against over 14.5, assuming normal settlement. Pending stays pending until the result and applicable rules are confirmed.

An example illustrates recordkeeping; it supplies no evidence that a real pick was published, placed or won.

Judge the process, not one result.

A sound explanation can lose, and a weak explanation can win once. Complete records help distinguish them over time.
