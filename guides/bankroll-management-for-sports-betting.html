BANKROLL DISCIPLINE

Make the stake boring.

A bankroll plan does not make betting profitable or safe. A preset budget sets a spending boundary; it does not remove the risk of losses or gambling-related harm.

By Kobe’s Betting Hub · Page updated October 22, 2026 · Sources checked October 22, 2026

Separate betting money first+

A betting bankroll should be money you can afford to lose without affecting rent, food, debt payments, savings, or other obligations. If losing the entire amount would create a real-life problem, it is not a betting bankroll.

The Responsible Gambling Council’s Plan Before You Play guidance recommends setting a time and spending budget and avoiding gambling to pay bills or replace income. This is harm-prevention guidance, not a promise that any budget is safe.

Use a defined unit+

A unit is a consistent reference stake, often expressed as a small percentage of the bankroll. It lets records and recommendations be discussed without assuming everyone has the same budget.

Example only: $1,000 bankroll × 1% = $10 per unit
A 0.5-unit play = $5 · A 1-unit play = $10

The 1% figure is an arithmetic illustration, not a recommended or evidence-backed safe stake. A “unit” is a recordkeeping convention, not a safety guarantee. For the same number of otherwise identical bets, smaller stakes reduce the dollars at risk; they do not improve the odds, prevent harm or guarantee that a budget lasts. Do not treat a depleted budget as a reason to add money.

Do not chase+

Chasing means increasing a stake primarily to recover a prior loss. It does not make a later win more likely. Outcomes can also be related; calling a wager independent requires evidence. A preset limit is a boundary, not a recovery strategy.

  • Set a maximum daily and weekly amount before betting.
  • Do not increase stakes because a previous play lost.
  • Do not count an ungraded wager as available bankroll.
  • Take a break when decisions feel urgent or emotional.
  • Use sportsbook deposit, wager, and time limits where available.

The National Council on Problem Gambling lists chasing losses and feeling unable to control gambling among warning signs. The Responsible Gambling Council advises against chasing and recommends breaks.

Track a complete ledger+

Record the event, market, line, odds, stake, sportsbook, timestamp, closing price if useful, and final result. Include losses, pushes, voids, bonuses, and fees. A complete ledger shows whether the process matches the story you are telling yourself.

Selected screenshots are not a bankroll report.A few winning slips leave out stake size, losses, deposits, and the time period. Judge performance with a complete record.

This ledger checklist is the Hub’s editorial method, not an externally certified reporting standard. Separate deposits and withdrawals from wager profit/loss; a deposit is not a winning result, and an unsettled wager is not a confirmed win.

Know when to stop+

Entertainment becomes a problem when betting affects finances, sleep, work, relationships, or your ability to stop. If that happens, pause and use exclusion or support tools. The National Council on Problem Gambling’s current support page explains warning signs and ways to get help in the United States. See our responsible-gambling resources for additional options.
