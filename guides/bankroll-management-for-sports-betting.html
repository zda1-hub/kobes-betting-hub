BANKROLL DISCIPLINE

Make the stake boring.

A bankroll plan cannot turn a losing bet into a winner. It can limit how much one decision—or one emotional night—can damage.

Written and reviewed by Kobe’s Betting Hub · September 25, 2026

Separate betting money first+

A betting bankroll should be money you can afford to lose without affecting rent, food, debt payments, savings, or other obligations. If losing the entire amount would create a real-life problem, it is not a betting bankroll.

Use a defined unit+

A unit is a consistent reference stake, often expressed as a small percentage of the bankroll. It lets records and recommendations be discussed without assuming everyone has the same budget.

Example only: $1,000 bankroll × 1% = $10 per unit
A 0.5-unit play = $5 · A 1-unit play = $10

There is no universally correct percentage. Smaller sizing reduces volatility but does not eliminate losses. Your limits should be conservative enough to survive ordinary losing streaks without requiring deposits or larger wagers.

Do not chase+

Chasing means increasing a stake primarily to recover a prior loss. It turns the next independent outcome into an emotional rescue attempt. A preset unit makes it easier to say no.

  • Set a maximum daily and weekly amount before betting.
  • Do not increase stakes because a previous play lost.
  • Do not count an ungraded wager as available bankroll.
  • Take a break when decisions feel urgent or emotional.
  • Use sportsbook deposit, wager, and time limits where available.
Track a complete ledger+

Record the event, market, line, odds, stake, sportsbook, timestamp, closing price if useful, and final result. Include losses, pushes, voids, bonuses, and fees. A complete ledger shows whether the process matches the story you are telling yourself.

Selected screenshots are not a bankroll report.A few winning slips leave out stake size, losses, deposits, and the time period. Judge performance with a complete record.
Know when to stop+

Entertainment becomes a problem when betting affects finances, sleep, work, relationships, or your ability to stop. If that happens, pause and use exclusion or support tools. In the United States, call or text 1-800-GAMBLER for confidential help.
